Emergency Septic Overview

When a Septic Problem Cannot Wait Until Morning

A septic system fails in one of two ways. Slowly, over years, in a way an inspection would have caught. Or suddenly, on a Saturday night, with a house full of people and wastewater coming back through the lowest drain in the building. The second kind is what this page is about.

An emergency is not only unpleasant. Raw sewage indoors is a health hazard, effluent surfacing in the yard is a hazard to children and animals, and every hour a blocked or overloaded system keeps running pushes solids further into the drainfield. What starts as a call-out can turn into a field replacement if it is left to sort itself out over a weekend.

What counts as a septic emergency

If wastewater is coming back into the house, standing on the ground, or an alarm is sounding on an aerobic unit, that is an emergency and it should be called in straight away. So is a broken or collapsed tank lid, which is a genuine falling hazard, and any backup that returns within days of being cleared. Slow drains and odor sit a step below that, but they are usually the same problem earlier in its life.

Elite Septic Service has worked on East Texas septic systems for 10 years. The crew that answers an emergency call is the same crew that repairs, pumps and replaces these systems, which means the person standing in your yard at nine at night can tell you whether this is a blockage, a pump, or the end of a drainfield.

What to do before we arrive

  • Stop using water in the house — no laundry, no dishwasher, no showers
  • Keep children and pets well away from any standing wastewater
  • Do not flush the toilets, even once, if sewage is already backing up
  • Do not pour drain chemicals into a backed-up system
  • If an alarm is sounding, note whether it is the pump or the high-water alarm
  • Leave the tank lid alone if it is cracked or open — it is a fall hazard

What We Handle

Septic Emergencies We Respond To

These are the calls that come in after hours, in the rain, and on holiday weekends. Every one of them is something we handle with our own crew and our own equipment.

Sewage Backing Up Indoors

Wastewater coming back through a tub, a shower pan, a floor drain or the lowest toilet in the house. It usually means the tank or the line out of the house has stopped clearing, and it will get worse with every gallon that goes down a drain.

Sewage Surfacing in the Yard

Standing water or visible effluent above the tank or over the drainfield. It is a health hazard as well as a system failure, and it needs the area kept clear of people and animals until we have looked at it.

Full or Overloaded Tank

A tank that has filled to the outlet, either because it is long overdue for pumping or because nothing is leaving it. Emergency pumping buys immediate relief and gives us a clear tank to work in while we find the real cause.

Blocked Sewer or Inlet Line

The line between the house and the tank can block with grease, wipes, roots or a collapsed section of pipe. We clear it, then look at why it happened, because a line that blocks twice is telling you something about its condition.

Aerobic Alarm Sounding

On an aerobic treatment unit the alarm is doing its job: high water, a failed pump, a tripped breaker or an air compressor that has stopped. Silencing it without finding the cause is how a nuisance becomes a backup two days later.

Failed Pump, Float or Control Panel

Effluent pumps, lift pumps and float switches fail without warning, and on a pumped or spray system nothing moves once they do. We diagnose the panel, test the floats and replace the pump where that is what it needs.

Broken or Collapsed Tank Lid

A cracked, shifted or collapsed lid on an older concrete tank is a serious falling hazard, particularly on properties with children or livestock. Rope it off, keep everyone away, and call us — this one genuinely cannot wait.

Drainfield Flooded After Heavy Rain

East Texas gets long wet spells, and a field with little capacity in reserve stops absorbing once the ground is saturated. Backups that only happen after several days of rain point straight at the drainfield rather than the tank.

Strong Sewage Odor Indoors or Out

A working system is not something you smell. Persistent odor points to a lost seal, a dry or broken trap, a failed baffle, or effluent reaching the surface — and sewer gas indoors is not something to live with while you wait.

Lines Damaged During Digging

Fence posts, driveway work, tree removal and new construction all find septic lines that nobody knew were there. If a line, a lid or a distribution box has been struck, stop work and call before anything else goes down the drain.

Once the emergency is under control, we handle whatever it turns out to need: septic tank repair, septic tank installation and replacement, pumping and ongoing maintenance across East Texas.

Who We Help

Emergency Septic Help for Homes, Land and Businesses

The system is the same; the pressure around it is not. Here is how emergency calls tend to look depending on who is making them.

Homeowners with a backup indoors

This is the call we take most often. The priority is getting wastewater out of the house and the system draining again, then finding out whether it was the line, the tank or the field. Stop using water the moment you notice it, and do not keep flushing to see whether it clears.

Rural properties and acreage

Older systems on rural tracts are often undocumented, undersized for the household now living there, and sitting further from the house than anyone remembers. Part of an emergency call out here is simply locating the tank and the field before anything can be opened.

Landlords and property managers

A backup in a rental is a habitability problem and a tenant relations problem at the same time. We can coordinate access with the tenant, document what we found with photographs, and give you written pricing you can act on without a second visit.

Restaurants, shops and small businesses

Commercial systems carry heavier and greasier loads than a household, and a blocked line usually means the doors close. We prioritise these calls and, once the immediate problem is cleared, look at grease, capacity and pumping intervals so it does not happen again next quarter.

Aerobic systems and alarms

Aerobic treatment units are common across East Texas because of the clay, and they bring pumps, floats, compressors, spray heads and a control panel that can all fail. Texas requires these systems to be kept under an active maintenance contract, and a sounding alarm is the unit telling you something in that chain has stopped.

After storms and flooding

Once the ground is saturated a drainfield has nowhere to send effluent, and marginal systems back up. Do not pump a tank while the ground is still flooded — an empty tank can float or shift. Call and we will tell you what is safe to do at that point.

Causes and Fixes

What Causes Most Septic Emergencies, and What We Do About It

Almost every emergency call has a cause that was building for months. Knowing which one you are looking at is the difference between a repair and a replacement.

What usually causes an emergency

  • A tank long overdue for pumping, with solids now reaching the outlet
  • A missing or broken outlet baffle letting solids through to the drainfield
  • Grease, wipes and non-flushables blocking the line out of the house
  • Tree and shrub roots grown into the sewer line, the trenches or the tank
  • A failed effluent pump, float switch or aerobic air compressor
  • A saturated drainfield with no absorption capacity left after heavy rain
  • A crushed, collapsed or struck line, lid or distribution box

Warning Signs

Signs You Need Emergency Septic Service Now

Any one of these on its own is worth a call. Two or three together means the system has already stopped coping, and the sooner it is looked at the cheaper the fix stays.

Wastewater coming back up a drain

Sewage appearing in a tub, a shower pan, a floor drain or the lowest toilet in the house. Stop all water use immediately — every load of laundry from here makes it worse, not better.

Every drain in the house slowing at once

One slow fixture is a clog. Sinks, tubs and toilets all slowing together is the system, and it is usually a few days ahead of a full backup.

An alarm sounding on an aerobic unit

The alarm means high water, a failed pump, a stopped compressor or a tripped breaker. Silencing it and carrying on is how a fixable fault becomes sewage in the yard.

Sewage standing on the ground

Visible effluent or standing water over the tank or the drainfield is a health hazard. Keep children and animals off it, do not hose it down, and have it looked at straight away.

Strong sewage odor indoors

A working system is not something you smell. Sewer gas inside the building points to a lost seal, a dry or broken trap, or a system no longer venting the way it should.

Gurgling toilets and plumbing

Air pushed back through the traps means the line or the tank is not clearing. It is one of the earliest warnings you get, and one of the easiest to keep ignoring.

A backup that returns after every pump-out

If the house drains fine for a fortnight after a pump and then backs up again, the problem sits downstream of the tank. That pattern points at the drainfield, not a blockage.

Backups that follow heavy rain

A system that only fails after several days of rain has almost no capacity left in the field. It is a warning shot, and the next wet spell usually collects on it.

A cracked, shifted or open tank lid

An old concrete lid that has cracked or dropped is a genuine falling hazard, particularly around children and livestock. Rope it off, keep everyone clear, and call.

Emergency Septic Service FAQs

Common questions about emergency septic calls across Lufkin and East Texas. If yours is not here, call and ask — we will talk you through it.

What counts as a septic emergency?

Anything where wastewater is going somewhere it should not, or where the system has stopped working entirely. Sewage backing up into a tub, shower, floor drain or toilet. My septic alarm is going off. What does that mean?

On an aerobic treatment unit the alarm is doing its job. It usually means high water in the pump tank, a failed effluent pump, a float switch that has stopped working, an air compressor that has quit, or a tripped breaker. The alarm can normally be silenced at the control panel, but silencing it is not fixing it — the tank keeps filling. Cut water use in the house, leave the panel alone otherwise, and call. Texas also requires aerobic systems to be kept under an active maintenance contract, which is worth checking at the same time.

Sewage is backing up into my house. What now?

Stop all water use immediately, keep everyone out of the affected rooms, and call. Do not flush again, and do not run anything that drains. Backups indoors are the calls we prioritise, because raw sewage in a building is a health hazard and every additional gallon spreads it. When we get there the first job is to relieve the system — pumping the tank down or clearing the line — and only then do we work out whether the cause was the line, the tank or the drainfield.

Will pumping the tank fix the problem?

Sometimes, and sometimes only for a fortnight. If the tank was simply overdue, pumping it solves the emergency and a sensible pumping interval keeps it solved. But if the house drains fine for a couple of weeks after every pump-out and then backs up again, the problem sits downstream of the tank and the drainfield is the thing that has failed. That is why we measure sludge and scum levels and walk the field rather than pumping and driving away. Clearing the symptom without answering the cause just books the next call.

Can you come out after heavy rain or flooding?

Yes, and it is one of our busier calls in a wet spell. Once the ground is saturated the drainfield has nowhere to send effluent, so systems with little capacity in reserve back up. One important caution: do not have a tank pumped while the ground around it is still flooded, because an empty tank can float or shift in saturated soil. Call and we will tell you what is safe to do at that point and what should wait until the water has dropped.
