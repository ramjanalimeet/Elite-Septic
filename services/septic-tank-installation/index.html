Installation Overview

What Goes Into a New Septic System

Every new system in Texas starts with a site evaluation. The soil is examined, the lot is measured for setbacks from wells, property lines and water, and the system is sized to the number of bedrooms and the expected daily flow. Only then can a design be drawn and submitted to the permitting authority for approval.

Once the permit is issued, the installation itself is usually a matter of days: excavation, setting the tank, laying the drainfield or installing the aerobic unit and spray field, connecting the house line and passing inspection before anything is covered.

Conventional or aerobic?

A conventional system uses a tank and a gravity drainfield and needs very little beyond regular checks. An aerobic system treats wastewater to a higher standard and fits sites a gravity field cannot use, but it needs power, a maintenance contract and inspections every four months. The soil and the lot usually make the decision; our guide to aerobic vs. conventional systems explains the trade-offs.

System Types

Septic Systems We Install

The right system depends on the ground, the lot and how the property will be used. These are the systems we install most often across East Texas.

Conventional Gravity Systems

A tank and gravity drainfield for sites with suitable soil and enough room. The simplest system to own and the lowest running cost.

Aerobic Treatment Units

Mechanical treatment with a spray or drip field for tight lots, poor soil or high water tables. Requires a maintenance contract under Texas rules.

Low-Pressure Dosing Systems

A pump tank doses effluent to the field in measured amounts when the best soil sits uphill of the house or the ground needs even distribution.

Manufactured and Mobile Homes

Systems sized and placed for manufactured homes, including land that has never had a system before.

Shops, Guest Houses and RV Pads

Additional or expanded systems for outbuildings, second dwellings and RV hookups, designed so the main system is not overloaded.

Lake and Rural Properties

Systems for lake lots and acreage where setbacks from water, wells and slopes shape the design from the start.

Step By Step

How a Septic Installation Works

From first visit to final inspection, a typical installation follows six steps. The permit stage takes the most time; the physical work is usually quick.

1. Site Visit

We walk the property, talk through the house, bedrooms and future plans, and look at where a system can go.

2. Soil and Site Evaluation

The soil is examined and the lot measured for setbacks, so the design is based on what is actually in the ground.

3. Design and Written Quote

You receive a system design and a flat-rate price before anything is built.

4. Permitting

We prepare and submit the application to the correct permitting authority and answer any questions it raises.

5. Installation

Excavation, tank, drainfield or aerobic unit and house connection, usually completed in a few working days.

6. Inspection and Handover

The system is inspected before it is covered, then we explain how to look after it.

How long does a septic installation take?

The permit stage usually takes the longest, from a couple of weeks to longer depending on the authority and the time of year. Once approved, most residential installations take two to four working days on site, a little longer for aerobic systems or sites that need clearing. Wet weather can delay excavation in low-lying ground.

Septic Tank Installation FAQs

Common questions about installing a new septic system in East Texas.

Do I need a permit to install a septic system in Texas?

Yes. New systems need a site evaluation, a design and a permit from the permitting authority for your area, which is either the TCEQ or a local authorized agent. We handle the application for you.

How much does a new septic system cost?

It depends mainly on the soil, the system type, the size of the house and site access. A conventional system on good soil costs the least; aerobic systems and difficult sites cost more. We give a flat-rate quote after the site evaluation. Our guide to septic system costs in East Texas covers the main factors.

What size septic tank do I need?

Tanks are sized by the number of bedrooms and the expected daily flow under Texas design rules, so larger homes need larger tanks and fields. We size the system during the design stage.

Can a septic system be installed on a small or wet lot?

Often, yes. Aerobic and low-pressure designs can fit lots where a conventional field will not, although some sites have real limits. A site evaluation tells you what is possible.

How long does a new septic system last?

A well-designed system that is looked after can last decades. The drainfield usually decides its life, so keeping solids, grease and vehicles off it matters most.

Do you install septic systems for new construction?

Yes. We work with homeowners, builders and contractors, and it helps to bring us in early so the system location can be planned alongside the house.
