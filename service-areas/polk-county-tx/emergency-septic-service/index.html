When It Cannot Wait

What Counts as a Septic Emergency in Polk County

Some septic problems can wait for a weekday appointment. These cannot: sewage coming up through a shower, tub or floor drain; effluent pooling on the ground; a tank or pump chamber overflowing; or a smell strong enough that something is clearly escaping. Each one is a health risk, and on a small lake lot each one can spread toward a neighbor’s yard or the water.

An aerobic alarm on its own is usually a warning rather than an emergency. It means a pump, float or compressor has stopped and the system is filling. If you can cut water use right down, it may hold until morning. If the alarm is joined by gurgling drains, a smell or water rising in the yard, call now.

What to do while you wait for emergency septic service

Stop running water. That means no showers, laundry, dishwasher or flushing if you can avoid it. Every gallon goes into a system that has nowhere to send it. Keep children and pets away from any wet or surfacing area. Do not open the tank lids yourself, and do not try to pump or bail the tank.

If the problem is on an aerobic system, check that the breaker for the septic panel has not tripped and tell us what the panel shows. If you are on a lake lot and effluent is running toward the water or a neighbor’s property, say so when you call so we can prioritize the visit.

What should I do with my septic system after heavy rain or flooding?

Use as little water as possible until the ground dries. When the field and the soil around it are saturated, the system has nowhere to send effluent, and extra water from the house just pushes it to the surface. Do not have the tank pumped while the ground is still flooded, because an empty tank in saturated soil can shift or float. If sewage is backing up or surfacing near the lake or a neighbor’s lot, call us, and we will tell you what can be done safely now and what should wait until the water drops.

Causes & Prevention

What Causes Most Emergency Septic Calls in Polk County

Most emergencies here have warning signs first. Knowing the usual causes helps you catch them early.

Common causes we find

  • A spray or effluent pump that failed
  • An aerobic compressor that stopped
  • A tank long overdue for pumping
  • A holiday crowd in a cabin-sized system
  • Saturated ground after heavy rain
  • Wipes or grease blocking the main line
  • An alarm that was silenced and forgotten

How to lower the risk of a Polk County septic emergency

  • Keep the aerobic maintenance contract active
  • Pump on schedule, not when it backs up
  • Spread out laundry before and during busy weekends
  • Never ignore or silence an alarm
  • Keep wipes, grease and fish waste out of drains
  • Cut water use during long wet spells
  • Have the system checked before a big gathering

Early Warnings

Warning Signs Before a Polk County Septic Emergency

Most emergencies give notice. Calling at the warning stage is usually cheaper and calmer.

An aerobic alarm that keeps returning

A recurring alarm means a part is failing. Fix it before it fails completely.

Spray heads not running

Quiet heads mean the pump chamber is filling. An overflow is coming.

Slow drains throughout the house

Every drain slowing at once points to the tank, the main line or the field.

Gurgling after flushing

Air pushing back through the traps means water is not leaving freely.

A smell around the yard

On aerobic systems, odor often means the compressor has stopped.

Soggy ground over the field

Wet ground when it has not rained means the field is struggling.

Drains slowing on busy weekends

If guests slow the drains, the system is near capacity.

Problems after rain

Trouble in wet weather near the lake means the field has little reserve left.

A lid that moves underfoot

A failing lid is a safety hazard. Call before it gives way.
