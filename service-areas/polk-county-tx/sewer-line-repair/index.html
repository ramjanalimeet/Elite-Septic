Line Repair Overview

When Another Cleaning Will Not Fix It

A drain that clears and stays clear had a blockage. A drain that clogs again in the same place every few months almost always has something wrong with the pipe: a cracked joint that lets roots in, a sag that holds solids, or a section that has been crushed. Cleaning removes the symptom, but the damage stays.

A camera tells us which it is and exactly where. That is the difference between digging one hole in the right place and trenching across your yard on a guess.

City sewer lines and septic lines in Polk County

In Livingston and Corrigan, many homes are on a city sewer, and the private line from the house to the city connection is typically the owner’s to maintain. In older parts of town, those lines may still be clay or cast iron, with joints that have shifted over the decades.

Around Lake Livingston and in the rural county, the line runs from the house to a septic tank. These are often shorter but shallower, and on lake lots they cross driveways and parking areas where boat trailers and trucks can crack them. We repair both kinds.

What We Repair

What Damages Sewer Lines in Polk County

These are the causes of line damage we find most often in Polk County.

Pine Root Intrusion

Roots enter through cracked or shifted joints and grow until they block the pipe and widen the crack.

Trailer and Truck Damage

Boat trailers and vehicles crossing a shallow line on a lake lot can crack or flatten it.

Old Clay Tile Joints

Clay pipe in older Livingston homes has many joints that separate as the ground moves.

Corroded Cast Iron

Cast iron rusts from the inside, thins and eventually cracks or collapses.

Orangeburg Pipe

Fiber pipe used in some mid-century homes softens and deforms with age until it fails.

Sags in Soft Ground

Where soil has settled or washed out under the pipe, a low spot forms and holds waste.

Offset Joints

When one section drops relative to the next, the lip catches debris and lets roots in.

Broken Tank Connections

The joint where the line enters the septic tank can crack or separate as the tank settles.

Storm and Tree Damage

A falling tree or uprooted stump can break the line nearby.

Poor Original Installation

Lines laid without proper bedding or fall are prone to sagging and repeat clogs.

Not sure if it needs repair or just cleaning? Start with a sewer camera inspection in Polk County. If the line is sound, drain cleaning may be all you need.

Step By Step

How We Repair Sewer Lines in Polk County

Find it precisely, fix it properly and disturb as little of your property as possible.

1. Camera Inspection

We run a camera through the line to see the damage and what caused it.

2. Locate and Mark

A locator pinpoints the damaged section, and we mark it on the ground with its depth.

3. Flat-Rate Quote

You get one price for the repair, with the footage to show why it is needed.

4. Dig Only Where Needed

We excavate the damaged section, working around driveways, decks and landscaping.

5. Replace and Bed the Pipe

New pipe goes in with proper bedding and fall, so it does not sag again.

6. Test and Backfill

We flow-test the line, backfill and leave the site tidy.

How long will we be without the sewer?

For a typical spot repair at a reasonable depth, the line is usually back in service the same day. We will tell you how long to avoid running water before we cut into the pipe. Longer sections, deeper lines or repairs under concrete can take longer, and we explain that in the quote. At a lake house, we can often do the work during the week when nobody is there.

Spot Repair or Replace the Whole Line?

A spot repair is often the right answer. It usually makes sense when:

  • The camera shows damage in one or two places
  • The rest of the pipe is in good condition
  • Roots enter at a single joint
  • A trailer or truck crushed one section
  • A sag is limited to a short stretch
  • The tank connection is the only failure
  • The line is PVC with one broken area

When the camera shows problems along the whole run, like corroded cast iron or Orangeburg pipe softening from end to end, a spot repair just moves the failure along the line. In that case we will tell you replacing the line is the better value and show you why.

Warning Signs

Signs Your Polk County Sewer Line Needs Repair

These point to damage in the pipe itself, not just a blockage.

The same clog keeps coming back

Repeat blockages in one spot usually mean a broken or sagging section.

Roots in every cleaning

Roots only get in through damaged joints or cracks.

A soggy strip in the yard

Sewage smell outside

A sunken spot over the line

Greener grass along a path

A tree came down nearby

An older home with original pipe

Sewer Line Repair FAQs for Polk County, TX

Common questions about sewer line repair in Polk County.

How much does sewer line repair cost in Polk County?

It depends on the length of damage, depth and what is above the line. We give a flat-rate price after the camera inspection.

Who is responsible for the line on a Livingston city sewer property?

The line from the house to the city connection is usually the owner’s. Check with the city for your property.

Can you repair the line without digging up my whole yard?

In most cases, yes. The camera and locator let us dig one targeted hole over the damage.

My boat trailer crushed the line. Can it be protected?

We can repair it and discuss options such as rerouting the line or changing where vehicles cross.

Is it worth repairing just one section?

If the rest of the line is sound, yes. If the camera shows damage throughout, replacement is usually better value.

What pipe do you use?

Modern PVC sewer pipe, bedded and laid with proper fall.

What is Orangeburg pipe?

A fiber pipe used in some mid-century homes. It softens and deforms with age, and a camera can identify it.

Do I need a permit?

It depends on where the property is and the scope of work. We confirm what applies before we start.
