Repair Overview

Repairs on Properties Nobody Was Watching

A pump fails at a deer camp in March. The alarm sounds to an empty property for weeks. By the time someone arrives in the fall, the pump chamber has overflowed, the field has taken months of untreated effluent and a small repair has become a large one. That pattern is the most common story we hear in Houston County, and it is why we take part-time properties seriously.

When we arrive, we open every chamber, check levels and test each component, because a system that sat failed for months may have more than one problem. Then we tell you what failed first, what that caused, and what needs fixing now versus what can wait.

Repairs on heavy soil and in the bottoms

Not every Houston County repair is a mechanical part. On the heavier Post Oak soil toward Grapeland and the Trinity, and on bottomland along both rivers, a field can stop accepting water in a wet winter while working fine in summer. Fixing a pipe will not change that.

When the symptoms follow the weather, we look at the field and the water table, not just the tank. Sometimes part of the field can be repaired or rested. Sometimes the honest answer is that the field is undersized for the soil, and we will explain what that means for you.

What We Repair

Septic Repair Work We Handle in Houston County

These are the repairs we make most in Houston County, on everything from family homes to seasonal camps.

Pumps That Failed Unattended

A pump that died months ago leaves an overflowing chamber and a stressed field. We replace the pump, clean up the overflow and check what damage followed.

Alarm Panels and Floats

Floats stick, alarms corrode and panels lose power on remote properties. We repair them and can add a remote or indoor alarm so the next fault is noticed.

Aerobic Compressors and Chlorinators

An aerobic unit that ran without air for a season has not been treating anything. We restore treatment and check the spray area for damage.

Long House Lines

Houses set far back from the road mean long runs to the tank. We trace the line and find the break, sag or root mass before excavating.

Root-Choked Pipe

Pines, oaks and sweetgums grow toward the wettest thing they can find. We clear roots, replace damaged pipe and advise on trees nearby.

Cracked Tanks and Lids

Old concrete tanks crack and lids break, letting groundwater in and effluent out. We repair what can be repaired and tell you when the tank is done.

Distribution Boxes

Settling tilts distribution boxes so one line takes all the flow and fails early. We level or replace the box.

Baffles and Outlet Filters

Missing baffles and clogged filters either let solids out or back the tank up. Both are quick repairs that protect the field.

Field Sections in Heavy Soil

On tight western soils, part of a field can fail while the rest works. We assess whether a section can be repaired, extended or rested.

Damage From Logging or Equipment

Timber work and heavy equipment crush shallow lines and tanks. We repair the damage and mark the system so it does not happen again.

When the field itself has failed, see septic system replacement in Houston County. If you are not sure what is buried, start with a sewer camera inspection.

Step By Step

How a Houston County Septic Repair Visit Works

On rural and part-time properties, a careful diagnosis is what keeps the bill to one repair instead of three.

1. Tell Us What Happened

What you saw or smelled, when the property was last used, and whether an alarm was sounding. If you are not on site, tell us how to get in.

2. Plan the Access

For properties down long tracks, we check the route and the weather so the truck can reach the tank without damaging your drive.

3. Open and Test Everything

We check levels in each chamber and test pumps, floats, alarms and aerobic parts, because a system left failed for months may have several faults.

4. Explain and Price

You get a plain explanation of what failed and what it caused, with a flat price for the repair. Nothing is replaced until you agree.

5. Repair

Most pump, float, alarm and line repairs are completed on the visit. Work on the tank or field is scheduled, and we confirm whether it needs a permit and from which authority.

6. Test and Report

We cycle the system to confirm it works, then send photos and notes, which is especially useful if you were not there.

Can you finish a septic repair in Houston County the same day?

Most mechanical repairs, yes. We carry common pumps, floats, compressors and alarm parts, so a failed component is usually replaced the same visit. Repairs that need excavation on a long line or work on the tank itself may take another day. Weather is the other factor here: after heavy rain, some tracks and river-bottom sites cannot take a loaded truck, and we would rather make the system safe and come back than tear up your road.

When Septic Tank Repair Is the Right Answer

Repair makes sense when a part has failed and the field is still sound. These situations usually call for it:

  • A pump or float that failed while the property was empty
  • An alarm that will not reset
  • An aerobic compressor that has stopped
  • A break or sag in a long house line
  • Roots in one section of pipe
  • A tilted distribution box
  • A cracked lid or missing baffle

If the field surfaces every winter regardless of what is repaired, or pumping only buys a couple of weeks, the problem is the field or its size for the soil. We will tell you that rather than repair around it.

Repair or Replace

Septic Repair or Replace: How We Call It in Houston County

Signs a septic tank repair will hold

  • One component failed and the rest test fine

  • The tank walls are sound

Warning Signs

Signs Your Septic System Needs Repair

These are the signs Houston County owners report most. On a camp, check for them on every visit.

A smell when you open the camp

A sewage smell on arrival can mean dry traps, but outdoors it usually means effluent is escaping or treatment has stopped.

An alarm sounding on arrival

An alarm that has been running while you were away means a pump or float fault that needs attention now.

Wet ground over the field in dry weather

A soggy field when it has not rained means the field is overloaded or a line has broken.

Slow drains on the first busy day

If drains slow as soon as the camp fills, the system may be at the limit of its capacity.

Gurgling toilets

Air pushed through the traps means water is not draining freely through the line or tank.

Lush green strips

Unusually green lines in the grass can trace a leaking pipe or an overloaded field trench.

A sunken spot near the tank

Ground settling over the tank can mean a failing lid or a collapsing old tank. Keep people away.

Problems every winter

Trouble that follows the wet season points to field saturation, especially on heavier soil or near the rivers.

Spray heads not running

On aerobic systems, quiet spray heads mean the pump, timer or power has failed.

Septic Tank Repair FAQs for Houston County, TX

Common questions about septic repair on Houston County homes and camps.

How much does septic repair cost in Houston County?

It depends on what failed and how far damage spread. A float or alarm costs much less than a pump, and both cost less than tank or field work. We diagnose, then agree a flat price before starting. Estimates are free.

Can you repair my camp while I am not there?

Yes. Give us access instructions, and we will diagnose, call you with the price, make the repair and send photos.

My alarm was going off when I arrived. What should I do?

Limit water use and call. An alarm on arrival usually means a pump or float failed while the property was empty, and the chamber may be close to overflowing.

Why does my system only act up in winter?

Usually because the field is in heavy soil or near a river bottom, and the ground saturates in the wet months. That is a field capacity issue rather than a broken part.

Does a septic repair need a permit here?

Replacing parts like pumps and floats generally does not. Work that alters the tank or field usually does. We confirm the permitting authority for your address and handle the application if one is needed.

Can you reach a property down a long private track?

Yes. We check access when you book, and if heavy rain has softened the route, we may wait a day or two so the truck does not damage your road.

How do I avoid this happening again at the camp?

Add an alarm you can hear or see remotely, and schedule a check before and after the busy season. See our septic system maintenance in Houston County page.

Is it worth repairing an old system?

If the tank and field are sound, yes. If the field fails every winter or the camp now hosts far more people than it was built for, replacement may be cheaper over a few years. We will tell you which.

Do you offer emergency septic repair?

Yes, 24/7. Stop using water, keep people away from any sewage and call +1 936-208-3153.

How do I book a repair?

Call or text +1 936-208-3153, or book online. Tell us the address, how the property is reached and what you are seeing.
