Replacement Overview

When Septic System Replacement Is the Honest Answer

A system that surfaces every winter, a tank that has cracked through, a field that refuses water two weeks after a pump-out, or a camp that has grown from two hunters to a dozen: these are the situations where we recommend replacement instead of another repair. Repairing a failed field only resets the clock for a few months.

We show you what we found and explain why. If a repair will genuinely hold, we will say so and save you the cost of a replacement you do not need yet.

A chance to site it properly this time

Many older Houston County systems were put wherever was convenient: next to the house, at the bottom of the slope, or in the low corner that floods every February. On a large tract, a replacement does not have to repeat that. A soil evaluation can find the best ground on the property, and the design can put the field there.

That matters more here than in most counties, because the soil changes so much. Heavier Post Oak soil toward the Trinity and sandier ground toward the Neches call for different systems, and choosing the right site can turn an aerobic job into a conventional one.

Step By Step

Our Septic System Replacement Process in Houston County

Texas rules set the sequence. We add the site search and the access plan.

1. Confirm the Failure

We inspect and explain why the system needs replacing rather than repairing.

2. Find the Best Site

A licensed evaluator tests the soil, and we look for the best ground on the tract, not just the old spot.

3. Confirm the Authority

We confirm which authority issues permits for your address, using TCEQ records.

4. Design and Permit

The system is sized for peak use and the planning materials submitted. State rules allow 30 days for review.

5. Install When the Ground Allows

We schedule around weather so equipment can reach the site, then build the new system and retire the old one.

6. Inspect and Record

The work is inspected before cover, started and tested, and the new layout is recorded.

Can we keep using the property during a septic system replacement?

Usually, yes. Because the new system is often built in a different spot, the old one can stay in use until the final connection, which typically takes part of a day. For hunting camps, we try to schedule replacements in the off-season so the camp is ready before opening weekend. If a replacement has to happen mid-season, we keep the old system pumped and usable while the work goes on.

Planning a Septic Replacement on a Houston County Property

These questions shape the replacement on most Houston County jobs:

  • Where is the best-draining soil on the tract?
  • Which side of the county's soil divide is the property on?
  • How many people use the property at its busiest?
  • Will new buildings be added in the next few years?
  • How will equipment reach the site in wet months?
  • Is the old field in bottomland or a low corner?
  • When is the property least used?

Answering these before design is what turns a replacement into an upgrade rather than a repeat of the original mistake.

Like for Like or Upgrade

Replacing Like for Like vs. Changing the System

On acreage there is usually room to choose. These are the factors that decide it.

When the same type of system makes sense

  • The soil is sandy and well-drained
  • The old system failed from age or neglect
  • Use has not grown since it was installed
  • There is room for a field in fresh soil
  • The site is well above the bottomland
  • Access for equipment is straightforward
  • A conventional system meets current rules

When the replacement septic system should be different

  • The soil is heavy Post Oak clay
  • The best site is in or near the river bottoms
  • The camp now hosts many more people
  • Extra cabins or a shop have been added
  • The best soil is uphill of the house
  • The water table rises close to the surface in winter
  • The old field was sited in a low corner

Septic System Replacement FAQs for Houston County, TX

What Houston County owners ask us when a system has reached the end.

Can the new system go somewhere better than the old one?

Usually, on acreage. We evaluate the tract and place the replacement in the best soil, away from bottomland and low corners.

Our camp system fails every deer season. Will a replacement fix that?

Yes, if it is sized for the crowd. We design camp replacements for the busiest weekend, not the yearly average.

Will heavy clay soil mean an aerobic system?

Not always. Heavy Post Oak soil needs careful design, and sometimes a larger field works. Where it will not, an aerobic unit is the reliable answer.

Who issues the permit for a replacement here?

Septic permits in Texas come from TCEQ’s local authorized agent, or the TCEQ regional office where none exists. We confirm the authority for your address before filing.

When is the best time to replace a camp system?

In the off-season, so the new system is ready and settled before opening weekend.

What if the track is too wet for your equipment?

We schedule around the weather. If needed, we keep the old system pumped and usable until the ground can take the machines.

What happens to the old tank?

It is pumped and properly decommissioned so it cannot collapse or hold water later.

How much does a replacement cost in Houston County?

It depends on soil, site, system type and access. We quote a flat rate after the evaluation, with no surcharge for distance.

How long does the whole process take?

Allow for the evaluation, design and permit review, which can take up to 30 days, plus a few days of installation.
